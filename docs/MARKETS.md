## Which stocks are we best at?

Six rules were run on 362 stocks. Each stock's result (Sharpe ratio above T-bills minus holding's) in 2003–2014 was compared with its result in 2015–2026.

**The best stocks for a rule did not stay best.** Rankings barely carried over (rank correlations near zero), and the previous top fifth did about as badly as everyone else. A list of 'stocks we are best at' drawn from the past is mostly a list of lucky stocks. For example, for the 10-month rule: NI (+0.31 then -0.09), KEY (+0.31 then -0.09), HST (+0.30 then -0.28), NOC (+0.30 then -0.04), GL (+0.29 then -0.29).

One trait carried over a little: trend rules lost less on more volatile, higher-beta stocks, but most stocks were still better held in every volatility group (10-month rule, share beating holding in 2015–26 by 2003–14 volatility: 22% vol: 8.2%, 27% vol: 12.5%, 32% vol: 15.3%, 36% vol: 29.2%, 46% vol: 21.9%). How smoothly a stock had trended predicted nothing.

## Caveats

- Stocks: only companies still listed today (survivorship bias).
- Markets: one history each; 2007–2026 includes only a few big trends in some markets.
- No rule was tuned here; the Faber portfolio is the published rule, unchanged.
