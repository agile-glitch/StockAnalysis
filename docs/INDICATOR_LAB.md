# Indicator Lab: can tuning indicator settings beat holding?

Generated by `python -m stockanalysis.validation.lab`. The same results, plus a playground to try any settings on any stock, are on the app's **Indicator lab** page.

## Bottom line

**None of the 172 settings across 8 indicators beat simply holding the same stocks on data they were not tuned on.** Several looked better in training and lost it on new stocks and later years: indicator settings overfit just like model weights.

For moving-average crossover (+0.11), rsi oversold bounce (+0.11), time-series momentum (-0.21), the ranking of settings in training barely predicted their ranking on unseen data, so picking the 'best' settings mostly picked luck. For the other indicators the rankings were stable, but even their best settings trailed holding.

## Method

Tactic settings were treated like model weights and fitted the machine-learning way:

- **Train:** 73 stocks, 2003–2014. Every setting in the grid scored; the best training Sharpe chosen per indicator.
- **Validate:** 57 other stocks (2003–2014 and 2015–2026) and the training stocks in 2015–2026.
- **Test:** 84 stocks used for nothing else, both periods, run once.
- **Trading:** one shared pot of money split across the stocks the tactic holds (max 10% each), the rest in T-bills. Signals at the close, trades at the next open, 0.1% per trade. Sharpe ratios use returns above T-bills.
- **Edge:** the tactic's Sharpe ratio minus that of holding the same stocks equally.

## Caveats

- Large, still-listed US stocks only (survivorship bias flatters long-only results, including holding).
- A grid of common settings, not every possible combination; more search means more overfitting, not less.
- Taxes not modelled; short selling not tested.
